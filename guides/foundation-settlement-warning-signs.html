Foundation Settlement Warning Signs in Houston Homes

Almost every house in the Houston metro shows some evidence of foundation movement, because the expansive clay beneath it swells and shrinks with every wet and dry season. That makes the useful question narrower than "does my home have cracks": it is which of the things you are looking at indicate structural settlement, which are cosmetic, and which are not foundation-related at all. This guide sets out how we read those signs on Jersey Village and Houston-area homes, and what each one usually means.

Two principles run through all of it. First, a pattern tells you more than any single symptom, because clay movement affects a house as a system: a settling corner shows up simultaneously in a door, a crack, and a floor reading. Second, change over time outranks severity at a single moment, which is why a dated photograph and a documented elevation reading are worth more than an opinion about how bad a crack looks today.

The Signs That Usually Mean Structural Movement

  • Diagonal cracks radiating from the upper corners of doorways and windows, the classic signature of a slab rotating out of plane
  • Stair-step cracking through brick mortar joints, following the courses in a descending pattern toward the settled corner
  • Horizontal cracks in a brick course, which indicate the veneer is being loaded in a direction it was never designed to carry
  • A gap opening between the brick veneer and a window frame, door frame, or garage door header
  • Separation where an exterior wall meets a patio, driveway, or porch slab, widening over successive seasons
  • Doors and windows binding at one specific corner, with a visibly tapered gap along the frame
  • Floors that read more than roughly an inch out of level across twenty feet on a documented survey
  • Cracked or lifting floor tile in a line or band across a room rather than at one isolated point
  • A distinctly hollow sound when tapping a section of slab, suggesting the concrete has lost support beneath it

The Signs That Are Usually Cosmetic

Not everything that cracks is structural, and Houston produces plenty of alarming-looking symptoms that have nothing to do with a foundation. Hairline vertical cracks in drywall, particularly above doorways and at seams, are typically the house responding to humidity and temperature. Narrow cracks along a slab's deliberate control joints are the joint doing its job. Nail pops in ceilings, minor separation at crown moulding, and paint cracking at trim joints are finish issues.

Seasonal timber movement causes the most confusion. Houston humidity swings enough to swell and shrink door slabs measurably, so a door that drags in late summer and closes cleanly in January is probably reacting to moisture. The distinction from settlement is whether it corrects: foundation-driven binding stays put through the seasons and concentrates at one corner, while humidity-driven binding comes and goes and affects the whole edge more evenly.

How to Check Your Own Home in Twenty Minutes

  • Walk the full exterior perimeter and photograph every crack with something in frame for scale, such as a coin or a tape measure
  • Note any place the brick veneer has separated from a frame or header, and measure the gap at its widest point
  • Open and close every exterior door and any interior door that has given trouble, noting exactly which corner binds
  • Roll a marble or a golf ball on hard floors in several rooms and note both whether it moves and which direction it runs
  • Check where downspouts discharge, whether any is within a foot of the slab, and whether the ground slopes toward the house anywhere
  • Look for trees with canopies within about fifteen feet of the foundation, since root systems draw substantial moisture from the clay
  • Tap hard-floor areas with a knuckle or a mallet, listening for the hollow note that indicates a void beneath the slab
  • Date every photograph and note, so a repeat pass in six to twelve months produces a real comparison

None of this replaces an instrument survey, and it is not meant to. What it produces is a documented baseline and a sense of whether the symptoms cluster on one side of the house, which is the single most useful piece of information you can hand to whoever inspects it.

Why Houston Clay Produces These Symptoms

The soil across most of Harris County is high-plasticity expansive clay, meaning it takes on water and increases in volume, then dries and gives that volume back. Through a wet spring it swells and lifts whatever sits on it. Through a drought summer it shrinks and withdraws support. Neither movement happens uniformly across a lot, because the shaded side of a house, the irrigated flower bed, the tree canopy, and the downspout discharge all change how much moisture that patch of clay holds.

Differential movement is what damages a structure. A slab that rose an inch uniformly and came back down would produce almost nothing visible; a slab whose east edge stayed put while its west edge dropped three quarters of an inch cracks brick, binds doors, and slopes floors. This is why foundation problems in this region are not seasonal complaints that pass, and why the repair has to address the moisture differential and not only the concrete.

When to Get an Elevation Survey

Three situations warrant measurement rather than monitoring. The first is any sign from the structural list above, particularly in combination, since a cluster of symptoms on one side of a house is close to diagnostic. The second is a symptom that has demonstrably changed: a crack that lengthened, a gap that widened, a door that used to close. The third is a purchase, because an elevation survey before closing is inexpensive against the cost of inheriting an active problem.

A survey settles what inspection by eye cannot. Readings on a numbered floor grid establish where the slab actually sits, and that is what determines whether the answer is foundation piering for genuine differential drop, void fill for concrete that has lost support without dropping, drainage correction for an active water source, or crack repair alone once movement has stabilized. Homes built here since the early 1980s are usually post-tensioned, which changes how any of that work is laid out, and pier-and-beam properties are assessed differently again through crawl space repair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which foundation cracks are serious and which are cosmetic?

Width, direction, and change over time matter more than the crack's presence. Hairline vertical cracks in drywall or a narrow crack at a slab's control joint are usually cosmetic. Diagonal cracks running from the corner of a door or window, horizontal cracks in a brick course, stair-step cracking through mortar joints, and any crack wider than a quarter inch point toward structural movement. A crack that has visibly lengthened or widened across two seasons deserves an elevation survey regardless of its width.

How out of level does a Houston floor have to be before it matters?

Most engineers in this region treat around one inch of differential across twenty feet as the point where investigation is warranted, though the rate of change matters as much as the number. A home that has sat an inch out for thirty years without progressing is behaving differently from one that moved half an inch in two seasons. This is also why a single reading tells you less than a documented survey you can compare against later.

Do sticking doors always mean foundation problems?

No, and this is the symptom most often misread in both directions. Houston humidity swells timber doors seasonally, so a door that drags in August and closes cleanly in January is probably responding to moisture rather than movement. The foundation signature is different: the door sticks at one specific corner, the gap along the frame is visibly wider at one end than the other, and it does not correct when the weather changes.

Should I wait to see whether the movement gets worse?

Monitoring is a legitimate choice for minor, stable symptoms, and we recommend it to Houston homeowners regularly. What makes it safe is documentation: a baseline elevation survey and dated photographs, so the comparison in twelve months is measured rather than remembered. Waiting without a baseline is not monitoring, it is guessing, and it removes the evidence that would have shown whether anything actually changed.

Can a plumbing leak cause what looks like foundation settlement?

Yes, and it is one of the more common findings we document. A supply line or drain leaking beneath a slab washes fine soil out from under the concrete, producing a localized rounded dip in the elevation grid rather than the broad tilt that seasonal clay movement produces. Piering that area without repairing the leak means the soil keeps washing out, which is why a competent inspection tests for it before quoting structural work.

What a Repair Costs Once Movement Is Confirmed

Numbers help put the decision in proportion. Crack repair and minor stabilization on a Houston-area home generally runs $2,200 to $5,500. Slab piering typically falls between $8,500 and $19,000 depending on pier count and the depth needed to reach load-bearing strata, and a large perimeter project combined with drainage correction can exceed $24,500.
