Soil Conditions Under Cypress Homes

The clay beneath Cypress swells when it takes on water and shrinks as it dries, moving vertically by inches between a saturated spring and a drought summer. A foundation only cracks when that movement happens unevenly across its footprint, which is why the position of trees, downspouts, and low spots in a yard usually matters more than the age of the house.

Newer subdivisions add a further factor. Drainage on a recently developed lot is often designed for the neighborhood's stormwater plan rather than for keeping moisture consistent around one particular slab. Young landscaping leaves large areas of exposed soil that dry quickly in July, then saturate in the next heavy rain. We see the resulting differential settlement in Cypress homes well under fifteen years old, and it is a soil condition, not a construction failure.

  • Interior elevation survey recorded as a numbered grid across the full floor plan

  • Drainage assessment covering grade, downspout discharge points, and standing water locations
  • Soil moisture readings at multiple perimeter points to locate wet and dry differentials
  • Written diagnosis separating active structural movement from settled cosmetic cracking
  • Itemized scope and pricing, with any optional work identified as optional

When Cracking Is Not Structural

Not every crack in a Cypress home needs piering, and we say so when it does not. Hairline drywall cracks above doorways, a stair-step mortar crack that has not moved in years, and minor slab surface cracks are frequently cosmetic, especially in homes that finished their initial settling a decade ago. The elevation survey is what separates the two: it shows whether the structure is currently out of level and by how much, rather than relying on the appearance of the crack.

Homeowners who have been told they need $15,000 of piering are entitled to that data. When our survey shows a home within tolerance, the honest recommendation is drainage work and monitoring, and that is what we quote. Frequently Asked Questions

Do you charge for foundation inspections in Cypress?

No. Inspections in Cypress are free and include an elevation survey of the finished floor, a perimeter walk documenting cracking and drainage, and a written diagnosis. You get the measurements whether or not you hire us, which means you can compare our findings against any other contractor's quote.

What does foundation repair cost for a Cypress home?

Crack repair and minor stabilization typically runs $2,200 to $5,500. Slab piering on a standard Cypress home commonly falls between $8,000 and $18,000 depending on pier count and depth. Larger homes with significant differential settlement, or projects combining piering with drainage correction, can reach $24,500 or more. Pier count is driven by the elevation survey, not by square footage alone.

How long does foundation repair take in Cypress?

Most residential piering projects take two to four days on site. Crack repair and drainage-only work is often a single day. Newer Cypress subdivisions with easy perimeter access tend to move faster than older properties with mature landscaping and tight side yards, which is factored into the schedule at quoting.

Why do so many Cypress homes have foundation problems?

Cypress sits on the same expansive clay that runs under most of northwest Harris County, and much of the area was developed rapidly on former agricultural land. Combine soil that changes volume with moisture, young landscaping that has not established consistent ground cover, and drainage designed for a subdivision rather than an individual lot, and differential movement becomes common within the first decade.

Is your foundation work in Cypress warrantied?

Yes. Structural piers carry a written transferable warranty that stays with the home if you sell, which matters in a market with as much turnover as Cypress. The document records the piers installed and the elevation the home was returned to, so a future buyer's inspector has real figures to compare against.
