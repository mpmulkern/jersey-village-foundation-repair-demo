Pier & Beam Foundation Leveling in Jersey Village, TX

Jersey Village Foundation Repair Pros levels pier and beam foundations across Jersey Village and the greater Houston metro. Older neighborhoods here and in Champions, Spring, and inner-loop Houston were built on pier and beam long before slab-on-grade became the default, and those homes move for a specific reason: the concrete pads under the piers sit directly on Houston's expansive clay, which lifts in wet months and drops in drought. Over enough cycles the pads shift, shims work loose, and the framing above them stops being level.

The advantage of a pier and beam home is that it can be brought back to level from underneath, without cutting into finished floors. The work is precise rather than dramatic: measure the actual elevation across the whole structure, find every failed support point, and rebuild the load path so the framing carries evenly again.

How We Level a Pier & Beam Home

Guessing at which piers are low is how a house ends up over-lifted in one corner and still sagging in another. We work from measurements at every stage.

  • Full elevation survey of the finished floor, recorded as a numbered grid, so the target level is a documented figure rather than an opinion
  • Crawl space inspection of every pier, pad, beam, and joist, with rot, insect damage, and failed shims marked individually
  • Moisture assessment to identify drainage problems or plumbing leaks feeding the movement
  • Written scope that separates structural necessities from optional improvements, with the cost of each
  • Staged lift using hydraulic jacks, raising in controlled increments so framing and finishes adjust gradually
  • Permanent shimming, pad work, and framing repairs locked in at the corrected elevation
  • Post-repair elevation survey compared against the original readings, delivered with your warranty documents

Why Houston Clay Is Hard on Pier & Beam Homes

The soil under Jersey Village is heavy expansive clay. It swells when it takes on water and shrinks as it dries, and the vertical movement between a saturated spring and a drought summer can be several inches. A slab spreads that movement across one large mass. A pier and beam home concentrates it at dozens of individual pads, each free to move independently, which is why the symptom is usually a floor that slopes in one direction rather than uniform settlement.

Crawl spaces make it worse when water is allowed to collect. Standing water under a house keeps the clay at maximum swell year-round and puts sustained moisture against wooden beams that were never meant to stay damp. That combination produces the two failures we find most often in older Houston-area homes: pads that have tilted out of position, and sill beams that crumble at the point of contact. Correcting elevation without addressing the water simply resets the clock.

Signs Your Foundation Needs Attention

  • Floors that slope noticeably, or feel soft and bouncy when walked across
  • Gaps between baseboards and the floor, or between crown molding and the ceiling
  • Interior doors that drift open on their own or no longer latch
  • Cracks at the corners of door and window frames that keep returning after patching
  • Visible daylight, sagging, or standing water when you look into the crawl space
  • A musty smell in rooms above the crawl space, which often means sustained moisture below

Frequently Asked Questions

How is pier and beam repair different from slab repair?

A pier and beam home sits on a wooden framework of beams and joists held up by piers over a crawl space, so the structure can be lifted and shimmed back to level from underneath. A slab is a single poured concrete mass, which has to be stabilized with piers driven to load-bearing strata instead. Pier and beam work is generally less invasive because our crew works in the crawl space rather than breaking through interior flooring.

What does pier and beam leveling cost in the Houston area?

Straightforward shimming and pad adjustment on a Jersey Village pier and beam home commonly runs $2,200 to $6,500. Jobs that also require sistered joists, replacement of rotted beams, or new concrete pads typically fall between $7,000 and $16,000. Full re-piering with structural framing repairs on a larger or badly settled home can reach $24,500 or more. Every quote follows a written on-site inspection.

How do I know my pier and beam home needs leveling?

The usual signs are sloping or bouncy floors, gaps opening between baseboards and flooring, interior doors that swing open or refuse to latch, and visible separation at the corners of door frames. In the crawl space itself, look for shims that have fallen out, piers leaning off plumb, or beams showing rot and insect damage where they contact a pad.

Will repairs damage my floors or interior finishes?

Almost all of the work happens in the crawl space, so interior finishes are usually untouched. Lifting a house that has been settled for years can reopen old drywall cracks or make a previously stuck door move differently, which is normal and cosmetic. We tell you before the lift which finishes are most likely to shift so nothing is a surprise.

Is pier and beam leveling permanent?

The structural work is permanent when the underlying cause is fixed at the same time. Wood shimming corrects the elevation, but if the crawl space stays wet from poor drainage or a plumbing leak, the pads will move again. Our scope always includes identifying the moisture source so the leveling holds instead of repeating in three years.
