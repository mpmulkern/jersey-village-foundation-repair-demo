Foundation Inspection & Elevation Survey in Jersey Village, TX

Every repair we quote starts with a documented foundation inspection, because a foundation cannot be diagnosed by looking at cracks. Jersey Village Foundation Repair Pros performs free elevation surveys for homeowners across Jersey Village and the Houston metro, recording where your floor actually sits, what is moving it, and whether it needs structural work at all. A meaningful share of the inspections we run end with no repair recommended.

That matters in a market where foundation work runs from $2,200 to well past $24,500. The difference between a house that needs piers and a house with old cosmetic cracking is measurable, and the measurement takes an hour. Getting it wrong in either direction is expensive: unnecessary piering costs money you did not need to spend, while ignoring genuine movement lets a manageable problem become a structural one.

How an Elevation Survey Works

We set a laser level or digital manometer and take height readings at fixed points across your floor, building a grid of numbers rather than an impression. The readings show the shape of the slab: where it is high, where it has dropped, how sharply it changes between adjacent points, and whether the pattern matches a recognisable failure mode. A perimeter drop that deepens steadily toward one corner reads very differently from a localised dip in the middle of the house.

The shape of the data drives the diagnosis. Gradual dish-shaped settlement across a whole slab usually means broad soil moisture loss and often responds to drainage work alone. An abrupt step between two adjacent readings suggests a void or a failing section that needs structural support. Edge lift, where the perimeter sits higher than the centre, is common in Houston after a wet season and frequently reverses on its own. None of these are distinguishable by eye.

Pre-Purchase Elevation Surveys

Buyers ordering a survey before closing get the most value from this service. A standard home inspection notes visible symptoms and usually recommends further evaluation by a specialist, which leaves you deciding on an unpriced unknown days before closing. And if the survey shows a house that moved years ago and has been stable since, which happens often in older Jersey Village and Houston neighborhoods, you gain the confidence to proceed without overpaying for a problem that is not active. Signs Worth an Inspection

  • Doors or windows that have started binding, or that no longer latch without force
  • Diagonal cracking running from the corner of a door or window frame
  • Brick or mortar separation outside, particularly stair-step cracking along the mortar line
  • Gaps opening between walls and ceilings, or crown moulding pulling away at a corner
  • Floors that feel sloped, bouncy, or hollow underfoot in a particular area
  • Tile or grout cracking in a line rather than at a single stress point
  • A gap widening where a patio, driveway, or porch meets the house

Any one of these can have a non-structural explanation. Several appearing together, or one that is clearly worsening month to month, is worth measuring rather than watching.

What Happens After the Survey

You receive the elevation data and our reading of it on the day, followed by a written report. If the house needs structural work, the report specifies which repair and why: foundation piering where readings show genuine settlement, slab void fill where concrete has lost support without dropping, or pier and beam leveling on crawl space construction. Where moisture around the perimeter is the root cause, drainage correction appears as its own line item.

If the house does not need work, the report says that too, and we suggest a follow-up survey in twelve to eighteen months so you have a baseline to compare against. That second set of readings is what converts a snapshot into a trend, and it is the single most useful thing a Houston homeowner can hold on an older property.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the foundation inspection really free?

Yes for a homeowner inspecting their own property, including the elevation survey and the written report. We charge for two specific situations: a pre-purchase survey on a house you do not yet own, which runs $350 to $600, and a formal engineer's letter when a lender or insurer requires a stamped document. Both are quoted up front, and neither obligates you to book repairs with us.

What is an elevation survey and why does it matter?

An elevation survey measures the height of your floor at points across a numbered grid, usually every six to eight feet, producing a map of how the slab actually sits. It converts a subjective impression that a floor feels sloped into recorded numbers. Without it, a contractor is guessing at pier count and placement, and you have nothing to compare against after the work is finished.

How much floor movement is normal in Houston?

Houston homes essentially never read perfectly level, and they do not need to. Differential movement under about one inch across a typical floor plan is usually tolerable, especially when it is gradual rather than abrupt. The readings that concern us are a sharp change over a short distance, a pattern that has clearly worsened between surveys, and any drop concentrated at a load-bearing line.

How long does a foundation inspection take?

Plan on sixty to ninety minutes on site for a typical single-story home, longer for two-story houses, pier and beam construction, or homes with a complex footprint. You receive the elevation readings and our diagnosis during the visit, and the written report with itemized pricing follows by email within one business day.

Should I get a foundation inspection before buying a Houston home?

It is worth doing whenever a general inspector notes anything about the foundation, the house sits on expansive clay, or you simply want the number before committing. A general home inspection reports visible symptoms. An elevation survey tells you whether movement is active and what correcting it would cost, which is the figure that matters during negotiation.
