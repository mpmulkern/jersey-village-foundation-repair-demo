Slab Void Fill & Mudjacking in Jersey Village, TX

Jersey Village Foundation Repair Pros fills voids beneath settled concrete and lifts sunken slabs across Jersey Village and the greater Houston metro, using injected polyurethane rather than traditional cement slurry. A void is the empty space left when clay soil shrinks or washes out from under a slab that has not yet dropped to follow it, and it is one of the most common findings on our elevation surveys.

Houston's expansive clay creates these gaps on a seasonal cycle. Soil swells through a wet spring, holds the slab up, then shrinks through a drought summer and pulls away underneath. The concrete spans the gap until something loads it, and then it cracks or drops suddenly. Filling that space early is considerably cheaper than piering the slab after it has failed.

How Voids Form Under Houston Slabs

Three mechanisms account for nearly every void we fill in this area. The first is clay shrinkage: soil that swelled with moisture gives that volume back as it dries, and the surface it was supporting drops away from the concrete. The second is erosion, where roof runoff, a leaking supply line, or a poorly graded lot moves fine soil out from under the slab edge and carries it away. The third is consolidation of poorly compacted fill, which shows up most often under additions, converted patios, and slabs poured over trenched utility lines.

Each mechanism calls for different follow-up work. Shrinkage voids point toward moisture management around the perimeter. Erosion voids mean the water source has to be found and corrected or the gap simply reopens. Consolidation voids are usually one-time events that stabilize once filled. Identifying which one you have is the difference between a repair that holds and a repair you pay for twice.

Why We Use Polyurethane on Clay Soil

Cement slurry mudjacking works, and it remains a legitimate method on stable ground. The problem on Houston clay is weight. A slurry adds significant mass directly onto soil that has already proven it cannot carry the load, which is why slurry-lifted slabs in this region often need a second visit within a few years. Polyurethane weighs roughly four pounds per cubic foot after expansion, so it fills the space without meaningfully increasing the burden on the ground below.

The practical differences matter too. Injection ports are small enough to disappear under a patch, cure time is measured in minutes, and the foam is unaffected by moisture once set, so it neither washes out nor breaks down when the soil goes through another wet cycle. On a home with finished flooring, the reduced disruption is often the deciding factor.

Void Fill Versus Piering: Which One You Need

These two repairs solve genuinely different problems and get confused constantly. Piering addresses a foundation that has moved downward, transferring the structure's load through steel piers to soil deep enough to be unaffected by seasonal moisture. Void fill addresses concrete that has not moved yet but has lost its support underneath. A slab can need one, the other, or both in different areas of the same house.

Our elevation survey settles the question with numbers rather than opinion. Where readings show meaningful differential drop at the perimeter, the honest recommendation is foundation piering. Where readings are close to level but probing finds unsupported concrete, void fill is the correct and much less expensive answer. Where an active water problem caused the erosion, we quote drainage correction alongside it, and homes with slab cracking already showing also need crack repair included in the plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does slab void fill cost in the Houston area?

Void fill and slab lifting on a Houston-area home generally runs $2,200 to $6,800 depending on the square footage over the void, how much lift is required, and how many injection points the slab needs. Detached concrete such as a driveway, walkway, or patio usually falls at the lower end. Interior structural slab work with documented elevation loss sits higher, and projects that also need piering are quoted separately because they solve a different problem.

Is mudjacking the same as polyurethane void fill?

They share a goal and differ in material. Traditional mudjacking pumps a cement slurry through larger holes to raise a slab. Polyurethane injection uses a two-part expanding foam through much smaller ports. On Houston clay we use polyurethane for nearly all residential work: it weighs a fraction of a slurry so it does not re-load the same soft soil that failed, it cures in minutes rather than hours, and the smaller ports are far easier to patch invisibly.

Will void fill stop my foundation from settling further?

Not on its own, and any contractor who promises that is selling the wrong repair. Void fill supports a slab across soil that has pulled away beneath it. If the deeper soil column is still losing support, the structure needs piers to reach stable strata. Our elevation survey determines which case you have before we quote, and some homes need both: piers on the settled perimeter and foam fill across the interior voids.

How long does the work take and can I stay in the house?

Most residential void fill jobs are completed in a single day, and you can stay home throughout. The foam reaches load-bearing strength within about fifteen minutes of injection, so floors are usable the same afternoon. Driveways and garage slabs accept vehicle traffic the following morning. There is no heavy excavation and no spoil pile on the lawn.

How do I know there is a void under my slab?

The common signs are a hollow sound when you tap the floor, a section of slab that flexes slightly underfoot, tile or grout cracking in a band rather than at a single point, and gaps opening where a patio or driveway meets the house. None of these confirm a void by themselves. We verify with an elevation survey and probe testing at the suspected area before recommending any injection.

Warranty on Void Fill Work

Injected polyurethane carries a five-year workmanship warranty covering the filled area against re-opening under normal conditions. That is a deliberately narrower guarantee than the lifetime transferable warranty on our piering work, and the reason is honest: piers reach strata that does not move seasonally, while void fill sits in the zone that does. If an uncorrected water source refills the void, the warranty on the injection does not repair the drainage fault that caused it, which is why we quote both together when the assessment calls for it.
