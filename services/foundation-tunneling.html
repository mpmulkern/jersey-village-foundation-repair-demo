Foundation Tunneling in Jersey Village, TX

Jersey Village Foundation Repair Pros digs hand-excavated access tunnels beneath slab homes across Jersey Village and the wider Houston metro, so that interior piers and under-slab plumbing repairs can be completed without opening up your finished floors. A tunnel starts outside the perimeter beam, runs beneath the slab to the work area, and is backfilled in compacted lifts once the repair is signed off.

The decision to tunnel is rarely about the tunnel itself. It is about what the alternative costs. Breaking through a slab from inside means removing flooring that often cannot be matched, running a jackhammer inside an occupied house, and then paying twice over to put the room back together. Reaching the same spot from outside leaves the interior untouched.

When Tunneling Is the Right Call

Interior Pier Placement

Access for piers required beneath the middle of a slab, where the elevation survey shows interior drop rather than perimeter settlement.

Under-Slab Plumbing Repair

Working room for a plumber to cut out and replace a failed drain or supply line beneath the center of the house.

Sewer Line Replacement

Full cast-iron replacement runs on older homes, where multiple sections are failing rather than one isolated break.

Homes With Premium Flooring

Properties with stone, imported tile, or continuous hardwood where interior breakout would destroy material that cannot be replaced cleanly.

Occupied Houses

Households that cannot vacate for an interior demolition, including homes with young children, pets, or respiratory sensitivities.

Combined Repair Projects

Jobs where piering and plumbing work meet in the same area and one tunnel can serve both trades in sequence.

How Our Tunnels Are Excavated

Every tunnel on a Houston clay lot is dug by hand. Machine excavation cannot be controlled precisely enough beneath a loaded structure, and the risk of undermining the beam is not worth the hours it would save. Our crews work with short-handled tools, remove spoil by bucket, and advance the face in measured increments while monitoring the slab above.

Tunnel dimensions follow the job. A plumbing access route is typically around three feet wide and tall enough for a technician to work lying down or kneeling. A tunnel carrying pier equipment needs more height and a wider working chamber at the pier location itself. Where the clay is saturated or the run is long, we install shoring as the excavation advances rather than waiting for a problem to appear.

Our Tunneling Process

  • Elevation survey and plumbing locate to establish exactly where the tunnel must terminate
  • Utility marking and confirmation of gas, electrical, and irrigation lines along the proposed route
  • Entry pit excavated outside the perimeter beam, sized for safe access and spoil removal
  • Hand excavation to the work area, with shoring installed where soil moisture or run length requires it
  • Repair work completed: pier installation, plumbing replacement, or both in sequence
  • Inspection and photographic documentation of the completed repair before any backfill begins
  • Backfill in compacted lifts with density checked at each stage, then surface restoration and cleanup

Why Backfill Decides Whether the Job Holds

The excavation is the visible part of tunneling, but the backfill is what determines whether the repair lasts. Soil returned loosely into a tunnel will consolidate under its own weight over the following seasons, and the slab above loses support exactly where you just paid to restore it. Homeowners who call us about new settlement a year or two after a tunneling job have usually inherited that problem from a contractor who rushed the closing stage.

We refill in lifts of six to eight inches, compacting each layer before the next goes in, and record the sequence on the completion report. Where the original soil was poor, select fill replaces it rather than being returned as-is. It adds a day to the schedule and it is the single step most worth paying for.

Tunneling Alongside Piering and Plumbing Work

Tunneling is an access method, not a repair in itself, so it is always quoted as part of a wider plan. Where the elevation survey calls for interior support, the tunnel delivers crews and equipment to the pier locations and the work is carried out as described on our foundation piering page. Where an under-slab leak has been washing out soil, correcting the water source matters as much as the structural repair, which is why drainage correction is frequently quoted with it.

Some homes need neither. If probing and survey readings show unsupported concrete but no meaningful elevation loss, slab void fill solves the problem from above at a fraction of the cost, and recommending a tunnel in that situation would be selling the wrong repair. Our elevation survey settles which category your house falls into before anything is quoted.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does foundation tunneling cost in the Houston area?

Tunneling is priced per linear foot of tunnel rather than per pier, and in the Houston metro it generally runs $175 to $325 per foot depending on soil conditions, tunnel depth, and how far the work must reach beneath the slab. A short tunnel to a single plumbing break might total $3,500 to $6,500. A long run serving several interior piers on a large home can reach $18,000 or more. We quote the footage after the elevation survey and plumbing locate, never before.

Why tunnel instead of breaking through the slab?

Interior slab breakout means jackhammering through your finished floor, removing tile or hardwood that often cannot be matched, living with dust and noise inside the house, then patching the slab and replacing the flooring afterward. Tunneling reaches the same point from outside the perimeter, so nothing inside the home is disturbed. On houses with expensive flooring, the tunnel is frequently the cheaper route once the restoration cost of breakout is counted honestly.

Will a tunnel under my house weaken the foundation?

Not when it is dug and backfilled correctly. Tunnels are sized to the work, kept narrow, and shored where soil conditions call for it. The critical step is the backfill: the tunnel is refilled in compacted lifts so the soil column beneath the slab is restored to its original bearing capacity. Poorly backfilled tunnels do cause later settlement, which is why we document compaction at each lift and note it on the completion report.

How long does tunneling take on a typical Jersey Village home?

Most residential tunnels take two to five working days to excavate, depending on length and whether the soil is dry clay or saturated. Pier installation or plumbing repair happens next, then backfill and compaction adds another day or two. A combined project usually spans one to two weeks on site. You can live in the house throughout, and water service is only interrupted during the plumbing repair itself.

Do I need tunneling for a plumbing leak under the slab?

It depends where the break sits. A leak near the perimeter can often be reached from outside with a short excavation. A break under the center of the house leaves two options: tunnel to it, or open the slab from inside. Many Houston homeowners choose the tunnel because the plumber gains room to work and the repair can be inspected properly, rather than being completed through a hole in the living room floor.
