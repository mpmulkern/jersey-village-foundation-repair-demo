Under-Slab Plumbing Leak Detection in Jersey Village, TX

Jersey Village Foundation Repair Pros tests for under-slab plumbing leaks before recommending any structural work, because a leaking line beneath a slab produces exactly the same symptoms as clay soil movement and responds to none of the same repairs. Hydrostatic testing, static supply testing, and camera location tell us which one is actually moving your home.

This is the step most homeowners never hear about until a foundation has been piered once already and the floor drops again. Water under a slab is a structural problem before it is a plumbing problem: it changes the moisture content of the clay the foundation sits on, and clay in this part of Harris County responds to moisture faster than almost any soil in the country.

Why Under-Slab Leaks Move Houston Foundations

Two failure modes, opposite in direction. A pressurised supply line weeping under one corner of the slab adds water continuously to soil that expands when wet, and that corner rises. It is a slow lift, usually a quarter inch to an inch across a season, and it shows in the elevation survey as a localised high point rather than the broad tilt drought produces.

A cracked or separated drain line does the reverse. Waste water discharging beneath the slab carries fine clay particles with it as it migrates, and over a year or two the soil column beneath that run loses volume. The slab ends up spanning unsupported ground, which cracks it in tension across the shortest path between supported points. On the elevation grid this reads as a rounded dip centred on the line, and it is the pattern we probe for whenever a survey dips near a kitchen or bathroom stack.

The practical consequence is simple enough. Piers driven into a slab with an active leak underneath will hold the structure at the new elevation, and the leak will carry on changing the soil around them. The homeowner pays twice.

Signs That Point to a Leak Rather Than Soil Movement

  • A water bill that has climbed without any change in household use, often the earliest signal on the supply side
  • A warm patch in a tile or wood floor, which indicates a hot-water line failure directly beneath it
  • The sound of running water when every fixture in the house is shut off
  • Damp or persistently dark flooring at one spot, or a musty smell confined to a single room
  • Foundation movement concentrated around a kitchen, bathroom, or laundry rather than spread across the perimeter
  • A home that was piered previously and has moved again in the same area
  • Soil at one section of the perimeter that stays wet through a dry Houston August

Any one of these on its own is worth testing. Two together makes a leak the most likely explanation, and we would not quote piering on that home without ruling it out first.

How We Test

The drain side is tested hydrostatically. We plug the main line at the cleanout, fill the system to slab level, and watch whether the water holds. A level that drops confirms a break somewhere in the sanitary system below the slab. Isolation testing then splits the system section by section, so the failure is narrowed to a run rather than left as a whole-house result, and a camera locates it precisely where the repair route depends on the depth.

The supply side needs a different method, because pressurised lines will not show on a drain test. We isolate the house at the meter, pressurise the system with air or water, and record whether it holds over a measured interval. A pressure loss with no visible fixture leak places the failure below the slab, and acoustic listening equipment tracks it to a location from the surface.

Both tests finish the same day. You get the readings, the isolation result, and a written statement of what was found, which is the document your insurer will ask for if the failure turns out to be a covered sudden loss.

What Happens After a Leak Is Confirmed

A licensed plumber makes the pipe repair; we provide the access and handle everything structural around it. Access is where the decision sits. Tunneling from outside the perimeter reaches the line without breaking the slab, keeps finished flooring intact, and lets the household stay in the house while work goes on. Cutting through the slab from inside costs less and moves faster, but it means removing flooring, breaking concrete, and repouring over the repair.

Once the pipe is sound, the soil question gets answered properly. Where a drain leak has washed material out, void fill restores contact between slab and soil. Where elevation has already been lost, piering takes the load to strata that seasonal moisture does not reach. A follow-up elevation survey a few months later confirms the readings have stabilised, which is the only honest way to say a repair worked.

Adding a static test on the supply side brings the pair to roughly $500 to $900, and camera location where the repair route needs it adds $250 to $500. Insurance is the reason to test early. Texas homeowner policies almost always exclude foundation settlement caused by soil movement, and almost always cover sudden discharge from a plumbing failure, including the access work needed to reach it. The distinction is decided on evidence, and evidence gathered after a slab has been opened is worth far less than a dated test result and photographs taken beforehand.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a plumbing leak under a slab actually move a foundation?

Yes, and it is one of the most commonly missed causes of movement in Houston. Expansive clay swells when it takes on water, so a supply line weeping beneath one corner of a slab lifts that section over months. A broken drain line does the opposite: it washes fine soil away and leaves the slab spanning a void. Both produce elevation change that no amount of piering will hold if the leak keeps running.

What is a hydrostatic test and does it damage the plumbing?

A hydrostatic test plugs the main drain line at the cleanout and fills the system with water to the slab level, then watches whether the level holds. It puts no more pressure on the pipe than a blocked drain would. The test is non-destructive and takes about an hour, though reaching the cleanout occasionally means pulling a toilet, which we reset afterwards.

Should I test for a leak before or after foundation repair?

Before, in almost every case. Piering a slab while a line is still leaking treats the symptom and leaves the cause running underneath the repair. Testing first also matters for insurance, since a sudden plumbing failure is often covered where soil settlement is not, and the documentation has to exist before anything is excavated.

How do you repair a leak once you find it?

There are two routes. Tunneling from outside the perimeter reaches the failed section without breaking the slab, keeps the family in the house, and is the usual choice where flooring is finished. Cutting through the slab from inside is faster and cheaper but removes flooring and concrete over the repair area. We price both and explain which suits the line depth and the room above it.
