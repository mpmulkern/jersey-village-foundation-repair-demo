Foundation Drainage Correction in Jersey Village, TX

Almost every foundation failure we repair in Jersey Village traces back to water: too much of it against one side of the house, too little under another, or the same ground cycling between both several times a year. Jersey Village Foundation Repair Pros installs drainage systems and root barriers that hold soil moisture steady around the foundation, which is the only way to make structural repairs last.

This is the least visible work we do and the most consequential. A homeowner who pays for piering without correcting drainage has bought a repair with a countdown on it. Fixing the water first, or at the same time, is what turns a foundation repair into a permanent one.

Drainage Services We Provide

French Drain Systems

Perforated subsurface drains in gravel-bedded trenches that intercept water before it reaches the foundation and carry it to a safe discharge point.

Lot Grading & Slope Correction

Regrading so the ground falls away from the house at proper slope instead of directing runoff back against the slab.

Gutter & Downspout Tie-Ins

Buried downspout extensions that discharge roof water well away from the foundation instead of dumping it at the corners.

Root Barrier Installation

Vertical barriers trenched between mature trees and the foundation to stop roots from drying out the soil on one side of the home.

Surface Drain & Catch Basin Work

Catch basins and area drains for yards that pond after Houston downpours and hold standing water against the structure.

Foundation Watering Guidance

Soaker hose layout and a written schedule to maintain consistent soil moisture through drought months.

Why Water Movement Damages Houston Foundations

The clay soil under Jersey Village and the surrounding Houston metro is expansive, meaning its volume changes with moisture content. Saturated clay swells and pushes upward. Dry clay shrinks and pulls support away. Neither condition damages a house on its own. The damage comes from the two happening in different places under the same foundation at the same time.

That is exactly what bad drainage produces. Runoff concentrating at one corner keeps that ground swollen while a large tree on the opposite side pulls the soil there into deep shrinkage. The foundation is then asked to bridge a differential that grows through every wet and dry cycle, and eventually it cracks, tilts, or settles. The fix is not adding strength to the concrete. It is removing the differential.

Our Drainage Assessment Process

  • Lot survey documenting existing grade, low spots, and the direction water actually travels during rainfall
  • Inspection of gutters, downspouts, and existing drains to find what is discharging at or near the foundation
  • Soil moisture readings at multiple points around the perimeter to locate wet and dry differentials
  • Assessment of mature trees and their distance from the foundation, including likely root spread
  • Elevation check of the structure so drainage findings can be matched against actual movement
  • Written plan naming the specific water source at each problem area, not a generic drainage package

Root Barriers Around Mature Trees

Houston lots frequently have large oaks and pines within twenty feet of the foundation, and mature trees like these draw hundreds of gallons of water from the soil on hot days. In sandy soil that hardly matters. In expansive clay it creates a persistent dry zone that shrinks and drops the ground on one side of the house.

A root barrier is a vertical panel installed in a trench between the tree and the foundation, deep enough to redirect the root system outward and away from the structure. Installed correctly it protects the house without harming the tree, which matters to most homeowners who would rather solve a foundation problem than lose a forty-year oak. We locate the trench based on the tree's actual drip line and the soil moisture pattern we measure, not a fixed distance.

Drainage Work Alongside Structural Repair

When a home already shows settlement, we sequence drainage and structural work so they reinforce each other. Structural correction through foundation piering or pier and beam leveling restores the elevation; drainage correction holds the soil conditions that keep it there. Scheduling both together also avoids paying twice for the same excavation and landscape restoration around the perimeter.

Homes with a crawl space often need drainage work inside the perimeter as well, which is covered on our crawl space repair page. Where cracking is present but movement has been limited, foundation crack repair combined with drainage correction is frequently the complete scope.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can drainage work alone fix my foundation?

Drainage correction stops the cause of movement, but it does not lift a foundation that has already settled. If your home has shifted and shows structural cracking, you need piering or leveling to correct the elevation, and drainage work to keep it from happening again. If movement is early and minor, drainage correction on its own is often enough to stabilize the soil before structural repair becomes necessary.

What does foundation drainage work cost in Houston?

A basic grading correction with downspout extensions commonly runs $1,200 to $3,500. A full French drain system around a foundation typically falls between $4,500 and $12,000 depending on linear footage, depth, and how difficult access is around the home. Root barrier installation is usually $2,200 to $8,000 depending on trench length. Combined drainage and structural repair projects on larger homes can exceed $24,500.

Do tree roots really damage foundations in Houston?

Indirectly, and significantly. Large trees pull enormous volumes of water out of the soil, and in expansive clay that localized drying causes the ground on that side of the house to shrink and drop. The roots rarely crack the concrete themselves; the damage comes from uneven soil moisture. A properly installed root barrier interrupts that pattern without killing the tree.

How deep does a French drain need to be near a foundation?

For foundation protection around Jersey Village homes, we typically install at 18 to 30 inches, below the zone where surface water moves laterally toward the slab. Depth depends on your lot's grade, the discharge point, and whether we are intercepting surface runoff or subsurface flow. A shallow decorative drain will move visible puddles but will not protect the foundation.

Should I water my foundation during a Houston drought?

Yes, and consistency matters more than volume. The goal is holding soil moisture steady so the clay neither shrinks nor swells sharply. A soaker hose 12 to 18 inches out from the foundation, run on a short regular schedule, is far better than heavy watering after weeks of neglect. Sudden saturation of dry clay causes rapid swelling, which is its own problem.
